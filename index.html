There is no simple mode and no pro mode.

A toggle would make you classify yourself before you know what you want. Instead the app grows per person, driven only by what you actually typed.

you typed · a name and a city

You get a live clock

Their time, ticking, next to their name. No availability, no green dot, no guess about whether they are at a desk. Adding a client takes about eight seconds and the app never invents a fact about them.

you also typed · their working hours

You get the whole thing

Around, winding down, off, away. Countdowns to the next shift. Notifications when someone opens or is about to close. Find a time, which intersects everyone's windows and tells you honestly when there is no overlap at all.

the rule underneath

A confident wrong answer is worse than none

Earlier builds assumed Mon to Fri, 9 to 6 for everyone. Add a client in Dubai and the app instantly called them off on a Sunday, from a guess you never made. Empty hours now mean unknown, and unknown shows nothing.

One line in the bar, and it picks the right one.

Menu bar space is the scarcest real estate on the machine, so the app never lists people up there. It runs a fixed priority ladder and shows the single most useful sentence it has.

Every rung is a unit test rather than something you catch by staring at your own menu bar at the wrong hour. If everyone is away and nobody is due back within two weeks, it goes quiet and shows only the icon, which is the correct answer.

Type where they live, not an IANA identifier.

Nobody searches for America/New_York. The zone list is the storage format, not the search format, so the search runs on 34,080 cities from GeoNames and 418 zones from the IANA table your Mac already ships, plus the handful of things no gazetteer contains, like uae and west coast.

Country names come out localised, so searching España finds Madrid. Every row on the right is a real query that used to return the wrong answer, and now does not.

The boring parts are the whole product.

Anyone can print a clock. What takes the work is the days it is not that simple.

1

Both DST transitions

A working window is built from calendar components, never by adding minutes to midnight. On the spring day that is 23 hours long, adding 540 minutes to midnight lands at 10am, not 9. The tests for this were written twice, because the first set passed against the naive version.

2

Weeks that are not Mon to Fri

Sunday to Thursday, the UAE four and a half day week, Saturday to Thursday, Sunday to Friday. Offered as a suggestion when you choose to add hours, never applied behind your back.

3

Saying there is no overlap

Find a time intersects everyone's windows across a two week horizon. When Auckland and Los Angeles genuinely never overlap, it says so instead of offering a slot that works for nobody.

136 tests · every one of them mutation checked · the engine holds no clock of its own, every function takes the instant it should reason about

It never opens a connection.

Not for time, not for cities, not for updates, not for analytics. There is no server to leak from because there is no server. Your list of people lives in one JSON file in your own Application Support folder, and you can open it, read it, back it up or delete it.
